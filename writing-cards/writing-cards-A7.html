1Descriptive Composition about a Person1/2

Structure

A descriptive composition about a person consists of three main parts: an introduction, a main body and a conclusion.

1. Introduction

We give some basic information about the person. We can mention:

  • His or her name.
  • When and where we met or saw the person.
  • How we know about the person.

2. Main Body

The main body describes the person in detail. It usually consists of separate paragraphs.

Physical Appearance: We describe the person's height, build, age, face, eyes, hair and clothes. We should start with general details and then mention more specific ones.

Personality and Character: We describe the person's character, behaviour and qualities. We can use examples to support our ideas. If we want to mention negative qualities, we should use polite expressions such as tends to, seems to, is rather or can occasionally be.

Hobbies, Interests and Daily Activities: We write about what the person likes doing in their free time, their hobbies, interests and everyday activities.

3. Conclusion

We give our personal opinion about the person. We can also express our feelings, explain why we admire them or say why they are important to us.

2Descriptive Composition about a Place or Building1/2

Structure

A descriptive composition about a place or building consists of three main parts: an introduction, a main body and a conclusion.

1. Introduction

We introduce the place or building. We should mention:

  • The name of the place or building.
  • Its exact location.
  • The reason why we chose to describe it.

2. Main Body

The main body describes the place or building in detail. We can write about:

  • Its size, appearance and surroundings.
  • The main features and different parts of the place.
  • The atmosphere and what makes it special.

We should use descriptive adjectives and adverbs to make our writing more interesting.

3. Conclusion

We express our feelings and final thoughts about the place. We can also give a recommendation and explain why other people should visit it.

The Five Senses

  • Sight: What can you see?
  • Hearing: What can you hear?
  • Smell: What can you smell?
  • Taste: What can you taste?
  • Touch: What can you feel?
3Descriptive Composition about an Object1/2

How to Describe an Object

We should give clear and accurate information so that the reader can easily imagine it. We should describe:

1. Size and Weight – how big, small, tall, short, heavy or light the object is.

2. Shape – round, oval, rectangular, square, triangular.

3. Pattern and Decoration – striped, floral, plain, patterned, decorated.

4. Colour – blue, red, yellowish, dark green, light pink.

5. Origin – where it comes from or was made: Chinese, Italian, Turkish, Japanese, Uzbek.

6. Material – cotton, wooden, leather, plastic, woollen, polyester, silk.

7. Other Details

  • Value and price.
  • Use and purpose.
  • Quality.
  • Special characteristics, such as a zip, strap, label, sticker or initials.

Points to Remember

  • Give accurate and clear information.
  • Use a variety of adjectives.
  • Follow the correct order of adjectives.
  • Do not use all adjectives in one sentence because it may sound unnatural.
  • Write information in different sentences.
  • Mention the object's use, quality, value and special features when necessary.
4Descriptive Composition about an Event1/2

Structure

A descriptive composition about an event consists of three main parts: an introduction, a main body and a conclusion.

1. Introduction

We introduce the event and set the scene. We should mention:

  • The name or type of event.
  • The time and place.
  • The reason for celebrating or organising the event.

2. Main Body

Preparations: We describe how people prepare for the event, including decorations, food, invitations, clothes and other activities.

The Event: We describe what happened during the event, the activities, people, music, atmosphere and important moments.

3. Conclusion

We describe people's feelings and opinions about the event. We can mention whether they enjoyed it, what they said and whether it was memorable.

Points to Remember

  • Use present tenses for annual events that happen every year.
  • Use past tenses for an event you attended in the past.
  • Use passive voice to describe preparations and activities.
  • Use narrative techniques to make the description more interesting.
  • Use a variety of descriptive adjectives to describe the atmosphere.
  • Describe people's actions, feelings and reactions.
5Writing Instructions, Describing Processes and Giving Directions1/2

How to Write Instructions

When writing instructions, describing processes or giving directions, we should give clear and detailed information in chronological order (the order in which things happen). We should use short, simple and clear sentences.

Where can we find them? Manuals, leaflets, cookery books, letters, guides.

Points to Remember

  • Give information in the correct order.
  • Use short and clear sentences.
  • Use the imperative form when giving instructions.
  • Write each stage on a new line.
  • Number the steps or use subheadings.
  • Use sequence words to connect the steps.
  • Use expressions such as make sure, remember, wait, be careful and do not.
  • Use the passive voice when describing processes.

The Imperative

Used to give instructions or commands. It usually starts with a verb.

  • Open the door.
  • Add some sugar.
  • Mix the ingredients.
  • Turn off the machine.
  • Do not touch the button.
  • Be careful!

Sequence Words

First · Then · Next · After that · Before · As soon as · Meanwhile · Until · Finally

6Narrative Composition1/2

What is a Narrative?

A narrative is a type of writing that tells a story. It can be imaginary or based on real-life experiences. It can be written in first person (I, we, my, our) or third person (he, she, they, his, her).

Structure

1. Introduction – set the scene and introduce the story: the people involved, the time, the place, and an interesting beginning to catch the reader's attention.

2. Main Body – two or more paragraphs. Develop the story: what happened first, what happened next, what problems or situations appeared, how the characters reacted.

3. Conclusion – finish the story: people's feelings and reactions, their comments, the results or consequences, an unexpected or surprising ending.

Points to Remember

  • Decide on the plot before you start writing.
  • Events must be written in the correct order.
  • Use time expressions to connect events.
  • Use different adjectives and adverbs.
  • Use direct speech to make conversations more realistic.
  • Use a variety of verbs to describe actions.
  • Add descriptions of people, places and objects when necessary.
  • Create an interesting and memorable ending.

Sequence Words

At first · Before · Until · While · During · Meanwhile · Then · After that · Suddenly · Eventually · Finally

7Letter Writing1/2

Types of Letters

Complaint · asking for/giving information · request · asking for/giving advice · invitation · accepting/refusing an invitation · congratulations, thanks, regret or sympathy · giving news · apology · job application · letters to the editor (suggestions or solutions).

Structure of a Good Letter

1. Greeting – Dear Peter, / Dear Mr Ford, / Dear Sir/Madam,

2. Introduction – explain why you are writing. I am writing to ask for some information. / I am writing to complain about a product.

3. Main Body – explain your ideas in detail, give necessary information, start a new paragraph for each main point.

4. Final Paragraph – summarise or say what you expect. I look forward to your reply. / I hope you will consider my request. / Thank you for your help.

5. Ending – closing phrase and your name. Yours sincerely, / Yours faithfully, / Best wishes, / Love,

Useful Expressions

Starting: I am writing to... / I would like to... / How are you? / I hope you are doing well.

Giving information: I would like to inform you that... / I am pleased to tell you that... / As you may know...

8Quick Summary – All Writing Types1/2

1. PERSON – Introduction (name, when/where met, how you know them) + Main Body (appearance, personality, hobbies) + Conclusion (opinion, feelings). Present tenses (living person), Past tenses (person from the past).

2. PLACE / BUILDING – Introduction (name, location, reason) + Main Body (appearance, size, surroundings, features, atmosphere) + Conclusion (feelings, recommendation). Adjectives, adverbs, five senses. Present (brochure), Past (past visit), Conditionals (ideal place).

3. OBJECT – size/weight, shape, pattern, colour, origin, material, value, use, quality, special features.
Opinion → Size/Weight → Age → Shape → Colour → Pattern → Origin → Material.
A beautiful small old round blue vase.

4. EVENT – Introduction (name/type, time, place, reason) + Main Body (preparations and the actual event) + Conclusion (feelings, comments, reactions). Annual events – Present; past events – Past. Passive voice, descriptive adjectives.

5. INSTRUCTIONS / PROCESSES / DIRECTIONS – clear information in chronological order. Short, clear sentences; imperative for instructions; number each step; passive voice for processes. First, then, next, after that, as soon as, until, finally. Make sure, remember, wait, be careful, do not.

Passive Voice

The passive voice is often used to describe preparations and activities.

  • Presents were wrapped in colourful paper.
  • The streets were decorated with flowers.
  • Delicious food was prepared.
  • Invitations were sent to all guests.
  • Traditional music was played.

Useful Vocabulary

Preparations: decorate, organise, prepare, invite, arrange, wrap, celebrate.

Atmosphere: crowded, cheerful, exciting, lively, festive, joyful, colourful.

Activities: dance, perform, sing, parade, compete, participate, entertain.

Feelings: excited, delighted, happy, proud, unforgettable, thrilled.

Useful Linking Words

  • First of all
  • Before the event
  • During the event
  • Meanwhile
  • Afterwards
  • Later
  • Finally
  • In conclusion

Example of Descriptive Language

During the carnival, onlookers line the crowded streets, clapping and cheering as they watch the brightly coloured floats and exotically dressed dancers go by. The sound of traditional music fills the air, creating a lively and unforgettable atmosphere.

7Letter Writing2/2

1. Formal Letters

Written to people we do not know personally, or to organisations, companies and official institutions.

  • Formal greeting: Dear Mr Smith, Dear Sir/Madam.
  • Formal language and polite expressions.
  • Frequent use of passive voice; complex sentences.
  • No contractions (do not use don't, can't, I'm).
  • Avoid slang and informal expressions.
  • Ending: Yours sincerely, / Yours faithfully,

Dear Sir/Madam,
I am writing to request some information about your courses.
Yours faithfully,
John Smith

2. Informal Letters

Written to friends, family members and people we know well.

  • Friendly greeting: Dear Alex, Dear Mum.
  • Informal, natural language; idioms and everyday expressions.
  • Contractions are allowed (I'm, don't, can't).
  • Short, simple sentences; friendly, personal tone.
  • Ending: Best wishes, / Love, / Regards, / Yours,

Dear Alex,
How are you? I hope everything is going well. I wanted to tell you about my new university.
Best wishes,
Anthony

Ending a Letter

Ending: I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your time. / I hope to hear from you soon. / Take care.

6Narrative Composition2/2

Tenses Used in Narratives

1. Past Continuous – background or situation at the beginning. It was raining and the wind was blowing.

2. Past Simple – the main events. Jonathan opened the door and entered the house.

3. Past Perfect – an action before another past action. He had planned the trip for months.

4. Participles – make descriptions shorter and more interesting. Startled, he ran towards the door. Smiling happily, she entered the room.

Useful Vocabulary

Feelings: excited, frightened, surprised, disappointed, disgusted, delighted, nervous.

Adverbs: fearlessly, surprisingly, suddenly, carefully, quietly, slowly.

Action verbs: shouted, whispered, rushed, grabbed, noticed, discovered, escaped.

Direct Speech

Shows exactly what a person says.

  • "What happened?" she asked.
  • "I don't know," he replied.
  • "Be careful!" shouted John.

Tips for a Good Narrative

  • Start with an interesting sentence.
  • Make the story easy to follow.
  • Use different sentence structures.
  • Describe feelings and actions.
  • Use suitable tenses.
  • Finish with a memorable ending.
5Writing Instructions, Describing Processes and Giving Directions2/2

Useful Expressions

  • Make sure...
  • Remember to...
  • Be careful...
  • Do not...
  • Wait until...
  • Keep doing...
  • Add...
  • Remove...
  • Turn on/off...
  • Follow the instructions...

Passive Voice in Processes

Often used to describe how something is made or how a process happens.

  • The peas are picked.
  • They are taken to the factory.
  • The peas are extracted from the pods.
  • The ingredients are mixed together.
  • The product is packed and delivered.

Example: How to Make Tea

  1. First, boil some water.
  2. Next, put a tea bag into a cup.
  3. Then, pour the hot water into the cup.
  4. Wait for a few minutes.
  5. Add some sugar or milk if you like.
  6. Finally, remove the tea bag and enjoy your tea.
